What Stash knows, shown as facts.

Codex's object intelligence gives every save a semantic kind, verbatim facts with the passage they came from, and a closed list of possible next actions. This page proposes how the details screen shows it: a facts section per kind in the details-tree style, each value opening its quote; the kind tag reading the semantic kind; and next actions as cells that are honest about what they still need. The data on this page is from your own saves except the recipe, which is a sample.

Four proposals

  1. A facts section per kind, labelled by the kind, in the tree the details drawer uses. Every value is a verbatim span; hover or tap opens the passage it came from, with the source named (the page, the transcript, the caption, the picture's text). Honest by design: nothing paraphrased is a fact.
  2. The kind tag reads the semantic kind (left), and the summary's topics sit under the section head as hairline tags.
  3. Next actions as cells from the capabilities list: a source-ready draft is an ink cell that makes a draft note (shopping list, recipe card, itinerary, study guide, flash cards); a lookup that needs a provider is a muted cell that names what it needs; an external write (calendar, grocery order) is dashed and always asks first.
  4. A footnote per section: "from the page · read Oct 10, 2026", and a "wrong kind?" link, because a misread (see the event example) should be one tap to correct.

What to decide

  1. Section placement: above notes (facts first) or above details (as drawn)?
  2. Should the quote open on hover, or only on tap (quieter)?
  3. Which drafts to ship first: shopping list and recipe card seem the clearest wins.
  4. Whether "wrong kind?" writes a protected override or just hides the section.
